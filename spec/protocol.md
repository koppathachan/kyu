# Kyu v1 — Protocol Specification (storage-provider subset)

> **Scope note.** This is the normative Kyu v1 protocol specification, reduced to the
> sections that bind a **storage provider**. Section numbers are unchanged from the full
> document so that cross-references inside the text remain correct.
>
> Omitted here, deliberately, because they are **out of scope for this work**:
>
> - §5 — Client API proto (`kyu.v1.QueueService`). That is the *engine's* contract. You are
>   not building an engine. A few provider-side comments below reference §5 types; the
>   equivalent storage-side types in §6 are the ones that bind you.
> - §8.2 — Engine conformance requirements.
> - §10, §11 — open items and change log.
>
> **What binds you is §8.1.** Those thirteen requirements are the definition of done.
> Nothing outside them is being asked for.

---

## 1. Scope

Kyu defines two contracts:

| Contract | Service | Parties |
|---|---|---|
| **Client API** | `kyu.v1.QueueService` | producers / consumers ↔ **Queue Engine** |
| **Storage SPI** | `kyu.storage.v1.StorageProviderService` | Queue Engine ↔ **Storage Provider** |

The engine is the only client of the SPI. Producers and consumers MUST NOT be given access to a provider. A provider is any component that implements the SPI over a backend (memory, relational, key-value, …) and passes §8.1.

The SPI is defined once, as proto. An implementation MAY expose it in-process (native function calls against the same method set and semantics) or out-of-process (gRPC). **Both bindings are the same contract**; conformance is defined against the semantics, not the transport.

This document does not specify: language, process topology, deployment, storage schema, or project organisation.

## 2. Model and terminology

- **Queue** — a named container plus its configuration (§5, `QueueConfig`). `queue_id` is an opaque client-chosen string, unique within a Kyu deployment.
- **Message** — payload bytes, optional string→string attributes, and an integer priority. Belongs to exactly one queue for its whole lifetime.
- **State** — `READY`, `LEASED`, or `DEAD_LETTER`. Exactly one at any time.
- **Delivery** — a `READY → LEASED` transition that returns the message to a consumer. Increments `delivery_count` and issues a fresh `lease_token`.
- **Lease** — a time-bounded exclusive right to settle a message, identified by the pair `(message_id, lease_token)`.
- **Settlement** — ack, nack, or forced dead-letter.
- **Engine** — the component serving the client API.
- **Provider** — the component serving the SPI.

**Identifiers.** `message_id` and `lease_token` are opaque strings, generated by the provider, at most 128 characters. Clients and engines MUST NOT parse them. `message_id` SHOULD be lexicographically ordered by creation time (UUIDv7 is RECOMMENDED) because it is the ordering tiebreak in §3.2.

## 3. Delivery semantics

### 3.1 Delivery guarantee
At-least-once. Exactly-once is a non-goal (§9); consumers MUST be written to tolerate redelivery. Enqueue-side deduplication (§3.7) suppresses duplicate *enqueues*; it does not suppress redelivery after lease expiry.

### 3.2 Ordering
Among messages eligible for delivery, providers MUST select in the order:

```
priority DESC, visible_at ASC, message_id ASC
```

`visible_at` is the time the message became (or becomes) visible: enqueue time plus initial delay, or the retry time set by the last nack. It equals enqueue time only for an undelayed first delivery — this is why `message_id` is the tiebreak and why §2 asks for time-ordered ids.

There is **no FIFO guarantee**. Concurrent consumers, retries, and lease expiry reorder delivery relative to enqueue. Strict ordering and message groups are deferred (§9).

### 3.3 Leases

1. Every delivery MUST issue a fresh `lease_token`. Tokens MUST NOT be reused across deliveries of the same message.
2. `RenewLease` and settlement MUST carry the token from the current delivery. A call carrying any other token, including a token whose lease has already expired, MUST fail with `FAILED_PRECONDITION` / `LEASE_LOST` and MUST leave the message unchanged.
3. `RenewLease` MUST NOT rotate the token. It extends `lease_expires_at` to `now + extension` (not "current expiry + extension").
4. `RenewLease` on a lease that has already expired MUST fail with `LEASE_LOST`, whether or not the message has been re-delivered yet. Expiry is final.
5. When a lease expires without settlement, the message MUST become eligible for delivery again with `delivery_count` **intact** (it was already incremented at delivery), subject to §3.4. The old token is dead from that moment.
6. A message in `LEASED` state with an unexpired lease MUST NOT be returned to any other consumer.
7. Reclaiming an expired lease is the provider's own responsibility. It MAY be lazy (at lease time) or by an internal sweeper. The engine MUST NOT depend on calling `ReapExpiredLeases` for correctness; that RPC is an administrative trigger only.

### 3.4 Delivery attempts and `max_deliveries`

`max_deliveries` is the maximum number of times a message may be delivered.

- `delivery_count` is incremented at the moment of delivery, and the value the consumer sees is the count *including* the current delivery.
- A message with `delivery_count >= max_deliveries` MUST NOT be delivered again. When such a message would otherwise become eligible (lease expiry, or a nack), it MUST be dead-lettered instead (§3.6).
- With `max_deliveries = 5`, a consumer sees a message at most 5 times.
- The transition to `DEAD_LETTER` MAY be observed lazily — i.e. it MAY be applied when the provider next examines the message rather than at the instant of expiry. Queue statistics MAY therefore lag by at most the provider's sweep interval. What MUST NOT happen is a delivery beyond the limit.

### 3.5 Retry and backoff

- A nack carrying an explicit `retry_delay` MUST make the message invisible for exactly that duration. Zero means immediately eligible.
- A nack omitting `retry_delay` MUST use the queue's policy, computed by the **engine**:
  `delay = min(retry_backoff_base × 2^(delivery_count − 1), retry_backoff_max)`
  The engine passes the computed value to the provider. Providers MUST NOT compute backoff themselves; they apply the delay they are given. (Rationale: policy stays in one place and providers stay trivial.)
- Implementations SHOULD apply jitter to the computed delay. If applied, jitter MUST NOT exceed ±20% and MUST NOT push the delay above `retry_backoff_max`.

### 3.6 Dead-lettering

- v1 has **no separate DLQ object**. A dead-lettered message stays in its own queue in `DEAD_LETTER` state, is never delivered, and is counted separately in queue statistics.
- If `dead_letter_enabled = false`, a message that would be dead-lettered MUST be deleted instead.
- A forced dead-letter settlement MUST move the message to `DEAD_LETTER` regardless of `delivery_count`, subject to `dead_letter_enabled`.
- A redrive API is deferred (§9).

### 3.7 Idempotent enqueue (deduplication)

- If `idempotency_key` is empty, no deduplication occurs.
- A save with a `(queue_id, idempotency_key)` pair whose dedup record is still within the queue's `dedup_window` MUST return the **original** `message_id` with `deduplicated = true`, and MUST NOT store a second message.
- The window is measured from the time the original message was saved, not from the last duplicate attempt.
- After the window expires, the same key MUST produce a new message.
- The dedup record is independent of the message's fate. A duplicate save inside the window MUST still return the original `message_id` and `deduplicated = true` **even if that message has since been acked, deleted, or dead-lettered.** Callers MUST NOT treat a returned `message_id` as proof that a deliverable message exists.
- Deduplication is scoped to a single queue. The same key in two queues is two messages.

### 3.8 Clocks

The provider's clock is authoritative for visibility and lease expiry. All timestamps in SPI responses are on the provider's clock, and the engine passes them through to clients unchanged. The engine MUST NOT make correctness decisions (e.g. "this lease is still valid, skip the renew") by comparing provider timestamps against its own clock; it MUST rely on the provider's `LEASE_LOST` responses. Clients SHOULD treat `lease_expires_at` as advisory and renew with margin.

## 4. Limits and validation

Implementations MUST enforce an upper bound on each of the following and MUST reject violations with `INVALID_ARGUMENT` / `VALIDATION_FAILED`. The values below are the defaults; an implementation MAY expose different limits but MUST document them.

| Parameter | Default limit | Behaviour on omission / zero |
|---|---|---|
| `Dequeue.batch_size` | 100 | 0 ⇒ 1 |
| `Dequeue.wait_timeout` | 30 s | 0 ⇒ return immediately |
| `lease_ttl`, `RenewLease.extension` | min 1 s, max 12 h | omitted ⇒ queue `default_lease_ttl` |
| `payload` size | 1 MiB | empty payload is legal |
| `attributes` | 16 entries, 4 KiB total serialised | — |
| `idempotency_key` | 256 bytes | empty ⇒ no dedup |
| `queue_id` | 256 bytes, non-empty | — |
| `Settle.settlements` | 1000 items | empty ⇒ `VALIDATION_FAILED` |
| `retry_delay` | max 12 h | 0 ⇒ immediate |

`priority` is a signed 32-bit integer; higher is delivered first; default 0. Values above `max_deliveries = 0` are invalid — `max_deliveries` MUST be ≥ 1.

Whether `attributes` counts toward the payload cap: they are budgeted separately, per the table above.

## 6. Storage provider SPI — `kyu/storage/v1/storage.proto`

Normative. Same codegen caveat as §5.

```proto
syntax = "proto3";

package kyu.storage.v1;

import "google/protobuf/timestamp.proto";
import "google/protobuf/duration.proto";

// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
// StorageProviderService — the swappable storage contract.
//
// The Queue Engine is the ONLY client of this service. A provider implements
// this contract over one backend (memory, relational, key-value, ...). The
// contract may be bound in-process or over gRPC; the semantics are identical
// and the conformance suite runs against both.
//
// Normative requirements (see the protocol spec for full statements):
//   - LeaseMessages MUST be atomic: under N concurrent callers, no message is
//     leased twice while a lease is live.
//   - Every delivery MUST rotate lease_token; Renew/Settle carrying a stale or
//     expired token MUST fail FAILED_PRECONDITION / reason "LEASE_LOST" and
//     MUST NOT modify the message.
//   - An expired lease MUST make the message leasable again (lazily at lease
//     time or via an internal sweeper) with delivery_count intact.
//   - A message with delivery_count >= max_deliveries MUST NOT be delivered
//     again; it is dead-lettered (or dropped if dead_letter_enabled=false).
//   - SaveMessage with a duplicate (queue_id, idempotency_key) inside the
//     queue's dedup_window MUST return the original message_id with
//     deduplicated=true and MUST NOT store a second copy.
//   - Providers MUST reap expired leases on their own; the engine never
//     depends on ReapExpiredLeases being called.
//   - Providers apply the retry_delay they are given; they do not compute
//     backoff policy.
// ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
service StorageProviderService {
  // --- Queue registry (durable queue config lives with the data) ---
  rpc EnsureQueue(EnsureQueueRequest) returns (EnsureQueueResponse);
  rpc GetQueueConfig(GetQueueConfigRequest) returns (GetQueueConfigResponse);
  rpc ListQueues(ListQueuesRequest) returns (ListQueuesResponse);
  rpc DeleteQueue(DeleteQueueRequest) returns (DeleteQueueResponse);
  rpc GetQueueStats(GetQueueStatsRequest) returns (GetQueueStatsResponse);

  // --- Message lifecycle ---
  rpc SaveMessage(SaveMessageRequest) returns (SaveMessageResponse);
  rpc LeaseMessages(LeaseMessagesRequest) returns (LeaseMessagesResponse);
  rpc RenewLease(RenewLeaseRequest) returns (RenewLeaseResponse);
  // Batch settle. Each item succeeds or fails independently.
  rpc SettleMessages(SettleMessagesRequest) returns (SettleMessagesResponse);

  // --- Maintenance ---
  // Administrative sweep trigger only. Providers MUST also reap on their own.
  rpc ReapExpiredLeases(ReapExpiredLeasesRequest) returns (ReapExpiredLeasesResponse);

  // Liveness/identity: the engine calls this at bind time and on health checks.
  rpc GetProviderInfo(GetProviderInfoRequest) returns (GetProviderInfoResponse);
}

enum MessageState {
  MESSAGE_STATE_UNSPECIFIED = 0;
  MESSAGE_STATE_READY = 1;
  MESSAGE_STATE_LEASED = 2;
  MESSAGE_STATE_DEAD_LETTER = 3;
}

message StorageQueueConfig {
  string queue_id = 1;
  google.protobuf.Duration default_lease_ttl = 2;
  int32 max_deliveries = 3;
  google.protobuf.Duration retry_backoff_base = 4;
  google.protobuf.Duration retry_backoff_max = 5;
  google.protobuf.Duration dedup_window = 6;
  bool dead_letter_enabled = 7;
}

message EnsureQueueRequest {
  StorageQueueConfig config = 1;
}
message EnsureQueueResponse {
  // If created=false the queue already existed and config is the STORED one,
  // which may differ from the requested one. The engine compares and decides
  // (see protocol spec §8.2).
  StorageQueueConfig config = 1;
  bool created = 2;
}

message GetQueueConfigRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
}
message GetQueueConfigResponse {
  StorageQueueConfig config = 1;
}

message ListQueuesRequest {
  int32 page_size = 1;
  string page_token = 2;
}
message ListQueuesResponse {
  repeated StorageQueueConfig queues = 1;
  string next_page_token = 2;
}

message DeleteQueueRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
}
message DeleteQueueResponse {
  int64 purged_message_count = 1;
}

message GetQueueStatsRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
}
message GetQueueStatsResponse {
  int64 ready_count = 1;
  int64 leased_count = 2;
  int64 dead_letter_count = 3;
}

message SaveMessageRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
  bytes payload = 2;
  int32 priority = 3;
  google.protobuf.Duration visibility_delay = 4;
  string idempotency_key = 5; // empty = no dedup
  map<string, string> attributes = 6;
}
message SaveMessageResponse {
  string message_id = 1;
  google.protobuf.Timestamp saved_at = 2;
  bool deduplicated = 3;
}

message LeaseMessagesRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
  int32 batch_size = 2;
  google.protobuf.Duration lease_ttl = 3; // omit -> queue default
}
message LeaseMessagesResponse {
  repeated LeasedMessage messages = 1;
}

message LeasedMessage {
  string message_id = 1;
  string queue_id = 2;
  bytes payload = 3;
  int32 priority = 4;
  int32 delivery_count = 5; // already incremented for this delivery
  string lease_token = 6;
  google.protobuf.Timestamp lease_expires_at = 7;
  map<string, string> attributes = 8;
}

message RenewLeaseRequest {
  string queue_id = 1;
  string message_id = 2;
  string lease_token = 3;
  google.protobuf.Duration extension = 4; // new expiry = now + extension
}
message RenewLeaseResponse {
  google.protobuf.Timestamp lease_expires_at = 1;
}

message SettleMessagesRequest {
  repeated StorageSettlement settlements = 1;
}
message StorageSettlement {
  string queue_id = 1;
  string message_id = 2;
  string lease_token = 3;
  Outcome outcome = 4;
  // Only meaningful for OUTCOME_RETRY; zero = retry immediately. The engine
  // has already applied the queue's backoff policy to this value.
  google.protobuf.Duration retry_delay = 5;
  string dead_letter_reason = 6;

  enum Outcome {
    OUTCOME_UNSPECIFIED = 0;
    OUTCOME_ACK_DELETE = 1;  // success -> remove permanently
    OUTCOME_RETRY = 2;       // nack -> READY after retry_delay
    OUTCOME_DEAD_LETTER = 3; // force to DLQ regardless of delivery_count
  }
}
message SettleMessagesResponse {
  repeated SettlementResult results = 1; // same order as the request
}
message SettlementResult {
  string message_id = 1;
  int32 code = 2;          // canonical google.rpc.Code; 0 = OK
  string error_reason = 3; // e.g. "LEASE_LOST", "MESSAGE_NOT_FOUND"
}

message ReapExpiredLeasesRequest {
  // Only reap leases expired for at least this long. Zero = all expired.
  google.protobuf.Duration grace_period = 1;
}
message ReapExpiredLeasesResponse {
  int64 returned_to_ready = 1;
  int64 moved_to_dead_letter = 2;
}

message GetProviderInfoRequest {}
message GetProviderInfoResponse {
  string provider_name = 1;    // e.g. "postgres", "memory"
  string provider_version = 2; // implementation version
  string contract_version = 3; // MUST be "kyu.storage.v1"
}
```

## 7. Error taxonomy

Both services use canonical gRPC status codes plus a machine-readable `reason` carried in `google.rpc.ErrorInfo`, domain `kyu.dev`. The engine maps provider errors to client errors 1:1 unless noted.

| Condition | gRPC code | reason | Retryable by caller |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown queue | `NOT_FOUND` | `QUEUE_NOT_FOUND` | no |
| Unknown message (already deleted or settled) | `NOT_FOUND` | `MESSAGE_NOT_FOUND` | no |
| Stale, rotated, or expired lease token | `FAILED_PRECONDITION` | `LEASE_LOST` | no — drop the work or re-dequeue |
| Queue exists with a different config | `ALREADY_EXISTS` | `QUEUE_CONFIG_CONFLICT` | no |
| Malformed request or limit exceeded (§4) | `INVALID_ARGUMENT` | `VALIDATION_FAILED` | no |
| Backend down / connection lost | `UNAVAILABLE` | `STORAGE_UNAVAILABLE` | yes, with backoff |
| Backend overloaded / rate limited | `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` | `STORAGE_OVERLOADED` | yes, with backoff |
| No provider bound for this queue | `FAILED_PRECONDITION` | `NO_PROVIDER_BOUND` | no |
| Provider rejects contract version at bind | `FAILED_PRECONDITION` | `CONTRACT_VERSION_MISMATCH` | no |
| Provider bug / unexpected state | `INTERNAL` | — | no |

**Batch rule (normative).** `Settle` and `SettleMessages` MUST NOT fail the whole RPC for item-level problems. The RPC returns OK with a per-item `code` / `error_reason`, in request order, one result per input item. The RPC itself errors only on transport failure or request-level validation failure (§4).

**Partial-failure rule (normative).** The engine MUST treat `UNAVAILABLE` or `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED` on `SaveMessage` as *unknown outcome* and retry with the same `idempotency_key`. Because providers deduplicate, this is safe. Clients get the same guarantee on `Enqueue` only if they supply a key; an `Enqueue` retried without a key MAY produce a duplicate message. Client documentation MUST state this.

## 8. Conformance

An implementation is conformant only if it satisfies both subsections. Conformance is defined behaviourally; §8.1 MUST be executable against any provider through either binding (in-process or gRPC) without modification.

### 8.1 Provider requirements

1. **Lifecycle** — save → lease → ack. The message is not leasable while a live lease exists, and is gone after ack.
2. **Deduplication** — the same `(queue_id, idempotency_key)` inside the window yields the same `message_id`, `deduplicated = true`, and exactly one stored copy. After the window, a new message. Also holds after the original was acked (§3.7).
3. **Lease expiry** — leasable again after TTL; `delivery_count` incremented on redelivery; the previous token is dead.
4. **Stale token** — renew and settle with a rotated *or expired* token fail with `LEASE_LOST` and leave the message untouched.
5. **Retry delay** — nack with a delay leaves the message invisible for at least that duration, then leasable.
6. **Dead-lettering** — a message reaching `max_deliveries` lands in `DEAD_LETTER`, is never delivered again, and is counted in stats. With `dead_letter_enabled = false` it is dropped instead. A forced dead-letter works below the limit.
7. **Priority** — among visible messages, higher priority is delivered first; ties broken per §3.2.
8. **Concurrency (the critical one)** — N ≥ 32 concurrent leasers against one queue of M ≥ 1000 messages, each acked exactly once: zero double-delivery while leases are live, zero lost messages, and the total delivered equals M.
9. **Batch settle** — a batch mixing valid and stale items returns per-item results; valid items are applied; invalid ones change nothing.
10. **Visibility delay** — an initial delay on save is honoured.
11. **Queue isolation** — messages never cross queues; deleting a queue purges its messages and reports the count.
12. **Reaping** — expired leases are reclaimed *without* any call to `ReapExpiredLeases`; when the RPC is called, its counts are consistent with induced expirations.
13. **Provider info** — `GetProviderInfo` returns `contract_version = "kyu.storage.v1"`.

## 9. Non-goals for v1

Exactly-once delivery; strict FIFO and message groups; live data migration between providers; dual-write or shadow traffic; DLQ redrive API; streaming consumer RPCs; transactions spanning queues; multi-tenancy, authentication and authorization (transport security and static tokens are targeted for v1.1); replication and HA of the storage backend, which is delegated to the backend.
